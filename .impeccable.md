## Design Context

### Users
Homelab enthusiasts and self-hosters who want the power of a reverse proxy without touching config files. They're typically comfortable with technology but not sysadmins — they want a GUI that makes complex networking tasks approachable. They configure things methodically but don't want to spend hours on it. They value "it just works" simplicity with the option to go deeper when needed.

### Brand Personality
**Friendly, sharp, modern.** Waygates should feel like a capable tool that doesn't intimidate. It's approachable without being childish, technical without being cold. Think of it as the friend who's really good at networking and makes it easy for you.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Reference**: Nginx Proxy Manager — functional, straightforward, gets out of the way. But Waygates should feel more modern and polished.
- **Anti-reference**: Overly complex enterprise dashboards (Cloudflare-level complexity), raw terminal-like UIs, or overly playful/gamified interfaces.
- **Visual tone**: Modern but simple. Clean lines, generous spacing, clear hierarchy. Not minimal to the point of being sterile — warm and inviting with purposeful color accents.
- **Theme**: Light and dark mode, both first-class citizens.
- **Typography**: Google Sans (display/body) + Geist Mono (code/technical values). Friendly and readable.

### Color Direction
Suggested palette direction — warm, trustworthy, with a distinctive accent:
- **Primary**: A warm teal or ocean blue (approachable, trustworthy, distinct from the typical purple/indigo AI slop)
- **Accents**: Amber/warm orange for warnings, green for active/healthy states, soft red for destructive actions
- **Surfaces**: Warm grays (not cold blue-grays) for backgrounds in both light and dark modes
- **Personality color**: A single bold accent (e.g., teal-500 `#14b8a6` or cyan-600 `#0891b2`) used sparingly for primary actions and brand moments

### Design Principles
1. **Clarity over cleverness** — Every element should be immediately understandable. No jargon without explanation, no hidden functionality. If a homelab user can't figure it out in 5 seconds, simplify it.
2. **Power through simplicity** — Complex configurations should be broken into digestible steps. Progressive disclosure: show the basics first, reveal advanced options on demand.
3. **Warm technical** — The interface should feel technical and capable but never cold or intimidating. Use friendly language, helpful empty states, and encouraging feedback.
4. **Confidence through feedback** — Users managing proxies need to trust the tool. Show status clearly, confirm actions, provide undo where possible. Never leave the user wondering "did that work?"
5. **Consistent rhythm** — Maintain visual consistency across all pages. Same patterns for search, same patterns for data grids, same patterns for forms. Predictability builds confidence.
